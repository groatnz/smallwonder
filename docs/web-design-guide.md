# Single-Page Infant Massage Website Design Guide

Mobile-first, scroll-based, connection-driven

1. Purpose and principles

Primary goal
The site’s main job is to get the visitor to contact the practitioner directly (call, SMS, WhatsApp, email, or enquiry form).

Secondary goals

- Communicate trust, safety, and professionalism
- Explain what infant massage is and why it matters
- Show who the practitioner is and why they’re credible
- Make class / session options clear enough to act on

Core design principles

- Mobile-first: design for a narrow viewport and touch interaction; scale up for desktop.
- Single scrollable primary page: navigation jumps to sections via anchors; external tools (e.g. booking widgets) may open in overlays or separate pages.
- Calm, simple, human: minimal visual noise, warm but clear language, strong focus on photos of parents and babies (with consent).

⸻

2. Information architecture for a single scroll page

This is the recommended section order for the main page:
 1. Hero / First screen
 2. Quick benefits & social proof snippet
 3. What is infant massage?
 4. About the practitioner
 5. Classes & options
 6. How it works (process)
 7. Benefits in detail
 8. FAQs
 9. Testimonials
 10. Final call-to-action & contact panel

Each of these is an anchor target for the top navigation.

⸻

3. Navigation and layout (single-page anchors)

3.1 Top navigation (mobile-first)

- Use a simple sticky header with:
- Logo / wordmark on the left
- Hamburger icon on the right
- The mobile menu opens as a full-screen or side-drawer list with anchor links:
- Home
- About
- Classes
- Benefits
- FAQs
- Contact
- All these scroll to IDs on the same page (#about, #classes, etc.).
- Include a persistent “Contact” button in the header (icon + label) that:
- Scrolls to the contact section; and/or
- Opens a small panel with “Call”, “Text/WhatsApp”, “Email”, “Enquiry form”.

3.2 Desktop behaviour

- Same anchor-based navigation across the top.
- Retain a prominent “Book now” or “Contact” button in the header.

⸻

4. Section-by-section content guide

4.1 Hero section (above the fold)

Goal: make it instantly clear what this is, for whom, and what action to take.

Content:

- Headline (1–2 lines, human, benefit-focused)
Example:
Gentle baby massage classes to help you bond, relax and feel more confident as a parent
- Subheading
Clarify that parents are learning, not outsourcing:
We teach you how to massage your own baby in a calm, baby-led way.
- Primary CTA buttons (two side by side on desktop, stacked on mobile):
- “Message [Practitioner name]” (for direct chat / WhatsApp / SMS)
- “View class dates”
- Support visual
- One strong image: practitioner or parent with baby, relaxed and fully clothed, clearly non-clinical.

Design:

- Full-height or 70–80% viewport height.
- Clear contrast; text legible over image (use overlay if needed).

⸻

4.2 Quick benefits & social proof snippet

Goal: reassure quickly and show others have found this helpful.

- Three short icon + text benefits, e.g.
- “Soothe and settle your baby”
- “Understand your baby’s cues”
- “Enjoy calm one-to-one time”
- One short trust line, e.g.
Certified infant massage instructor, running small group classes on Waiheke.
- Optional: star rating or one short testimonial.

⸻

4.3 “What is infant massage?” section

Goal: explain in plain language, without jargon.

- Short paragraph explaining that infant massage is:
- gentle touch,
- done by parents,
- to support bonding, comfort, and development.
- 3–5 bullet points clarifying what it is not:
- Not deep tissue
- Not a replacement for medical care
- Not forced if baby is upset

Include a secondary CTA: “Is this right for my baby? Message me”.

⸻

4.4 About the practitioner

Goal: build trust and personal connection.

Structure:

- Photo of practitioner (friendly, casual-professional).
- Short bio (2–3 short paragraphs):
- Background (e.g. midwife, massage therapist, parent)
- Qualifications and infant massage training
- Motivation: why they care about supporting parents
- Credentials list (compact bullet list):
- Certification (e.g. IAIM, IMIS)
- Professional memberships
- First aid / CPR

Include a micro-CTA at the end:

“Any questions about my training or approach? Get in touch.”

⸻

4.5 Classes & options

Goal: make it easy to understand what’s on offer and choose something.

Layout:

- Card-based layout (stacked vertically on mobile):
- Group course
- Private session (home visit or clinic)
- Special options (e.g. partners’ session, twins, NICU graduates) if relevant

Each card should contain:

- Who it’s for
- Duration and structure (e.g. “4 weekly sessions, 75 minutes each”)
- Location(s)
- Price (or “from $X”)
- What’s included (oil, notes, small group, follow-up support)

Each card ends with a small CTA:

- “Enquire about this course”
- “Check availability”

On desktop, show one line of reassurance near the cards:

“If you’re not sure which option suits your family, message me and we’ll work it out.”

⸻

4.6 “How it works” (process section)

Goal: reduce anxiety by showing the shape of a typical session/course.

Use a simple 3–4 step timeline, for example:
 1. We chat first
You tell me about your baby and any special considerations.
 2. Come as you are
We meet in a warm, relaxed space; babies can feed, cry, sleep.
 3. You learn at your baby’s pace
I demonstrate on a doll; you massage your baby only when they’re happy to.
 4. You take it home
You get notes/oil and keep practising the routine in your own time.

Include one line emphasising flexibility:

“If your baby sleeps or cries through a session, that’s normal. We adapt.”

⸻

4.7 Benefits in detail

Goal: give a deeper rationale for those who want it, without becoming medicalised.

Use two subsections:

- For your baby
- Calm and relaxation
- Comfort with touch and body awareness
- Possible support for wind/colic and sleep routines (careful language: “may help”, “many parents report”)
- For you
- More confidence handling and soothing your baby
- A regular, calm ritual together
- Chance to talk with other parents (for group classes)

Add a small “evidence / caveat” line:

Infant massage can be a supportive part of your baby’s care, but it doesn’t replace medical advice. If you have health concerns, please speak to your midwife, GP or paediatrician.

⸻

4.8 FAQs

Goal: pre-empt anxieties and objections.

Organise FAQs in an accordion (ideal for mobile):

- When can we start?
- What if my baby cries or sleeps during class?
- Can both parents come?
- What do I need to bring?
- What if my baby has reflux/colic or a medical condition?
- What oil do you use? Can I use my own?

Finish with a fallback CTA:

“If your question isn’t listed, send me a quick message and I’ll reply personally.”

⸻

4.9 Testimonials

Goal: use social proof close to the bottom of the decision journey.

- 3–6 short parent quotes, each 1–3 sentences.
- Ideally cover:
- increased confidence,
- baby seeming more relaxed,
- enjoying the group or 1:1 environment.

Optional: first name + suburb (“Sara – Waiheke Island”).

⸻

4.10 Final CTA & contact panel (footer area)

Goal: a clear “last chance” to act after scrolling.

Combine:

- Short encouragement line, e.g.
Ready to learn baby massage or still unsure if it’s for you? Let’s talk.
- Contact methods, each as a button / pill:
- Call
- Text / WhatsApp
- Email
- Enquiry form (embedded or modal)

On mobile, this section should be very simple and touch-friendly; avoid long forms.

⸻

5. Contact tools and behaviour

Although the main content is single-page, contact can use separate tools/pages where needed:

- Form behaviour
- Keep fields minimal: name, baby’s age, contact method, message.
- Use inline validation, clear success states (“Thank you, I’ll respond within X hours”).
- Messaging integration
- Click-to-call links (tel:)
- Click-to-WhatsApp or SMS (sms:) on mobile
- Mailto link that opens user’s email client
- Consider a floating “Contact” button on mobile bottom-right that always scrolls back to the contact panel or opens it.

⸻

6. Visual and content style

6.1 Visual

- Colour palette
- Soft neutrals with 1–2 muted accent colours.
- High contrast for text; avoid pale grey on white body text.
- Typography
- One clean sans-serif for body and UI, optionally a softer headline font.
- Base 16–18px on mobile; headings +4–8px above body.
- Imagery
- Real photos where possible (with consent).
- Avoid overly posed, stock-photo clichés.
- Always show babies clothed or wrapped in non-sexual, nurturing contexts.

6.2 Tone of voice

- Plain, warm English.
- Speak to one parent (“you and your baby”), not “clients” in the abstract.
- Avoid medical claims; use gentle language like “may support”, “can help”, “many parents notice”.

⸻

7. Mobile-first interaction details

- Tap targets at least 44px high.
- Generous spacing between buttons.
- Section spacing tuned so scrolling feels smooth and not cramped.
- Avoid large text blocks; break into short paragraphs and bullets.
- Defer heavy images; use responsive images to keep performance decent on slower mobile data.

⸻

8. Conversion strategy summary

The system is designed to:
 1. Clarify quickly what is offered and who it’s for (hero).
 2. Reassure fast with visible benefits and social proof.
 3. Deepen trust via practitioner story and credentials.
 4. Make choices easy with simple class options.
 5. Remove friction by answering common questions inline.
 6. Offer multiple low-friction contact methods throughout the scroll, not just at the bottom.

Treat every major section as a chance to gently say, in one form or another:

“If this sounds like it might help you and your baby, get in touch and we can talk it through.”

That single message, repeated in different guises, is what turns a pretty scroll page into an actual appointment-making machine.
