# About Infant Massage New Zealand (IAIM NZ)

## Who We Are

Infant Massage New Zealand (IAIM NZ) serves as the New Zealand chapter of the International Association of Infant Massage (IAIM), a global non-profit organization dedicated to promoting nurturing touch and communication through education, training, and research. Established as a registered charity (CC34350), IAIM NZ focuses on empowering parents, caregivers, and professionals to foster loving, respectful interactions with infants via standardized infant massage techniques.

The organization emphasizes consistency across its international network, with chapters in over 30 countries, including sister chapters in Singapore, Israel, Portugal, and the United Kingdom. IAIM NZ operates as an autonomous chapter, facilitating local trainings while adhering to global standards, ensuring that certified instructors worldwide share the same evidence-based techniques and philosophy.

## Our Mission

IAIM NZ's core mission aligns with the global IAIM: "to promote nurturing touch and communication through training, education and research so that parents, caregivers and children are loved, valued and respected throughout the world community."

The organization prioritizes respecting babies and supporting families by sharing evidence-based techniques that enhance bonding, relieve discomfort (e.g., colic, gas), and promote relaxation and sleep. Research integration is a key pillar, with references to studies from institutions like the Touch Research Institute, highlighting benefits such as improved interaction, stimulation, and emotional development.

## Our History

Founded by Vimala McClure in 1976, IAIM developed its curriculum from a blend of Indian and Swedish massage strokes, reflexology, and yoga principles, formalized in McClure's book *Infant Massage: A Handbook for Loving Parents*. The first instructor was certified in 1978, and IAIM has since expanded internationally as a non-profit organization.

IAIM NZ is governed by certified trainers like P-J McCrea and maintains a commitment to sustainability and global portability of certifications, allowing instructors to teach worldwide. The organization's administrative office is located at PO Box 32048, Maungaraki, Lower Hutt 5050, New Zealand.

## Credentials and Certification

### Certified Infant Massage Instructor (CIMI)

Upon successful completion of training and assessment, participants receive IAIM certification as a Certified Infant Massage Instructor (CIMI). This credential is globally recognized and portable, meaning certified instructors can teach parents and carers worldwide, contributing to IAIM's mission in diverse settings including workplaces, communities, and private practice.

### Training Standards

Infant Massage Instructors trained through IAIM have been through a comprehensive process of training and coursework before they become certified as an instructor. All certified instructors learn the same standardized techniques for nurturing, respectful communication, ensuring consistency with IAIM's global curriculum. Training is facilitated by approved IAIM Trainers, ensuring adherence to international objectives outlined in the IAIM Instructors Training Outline.

The curriculum covers theory and practical application for working with typically developing infants, those with colic or tummy pain, premature babies, and special needs children. Certified instructors are equipped with knowledge of behavioral cues, relaxation techniques, and parent connection strategies.

## What Instructors Do

### Teaching Approach

Certified Infant Massage Instructors offer a 5-week course or a series of individual sessions to teach parents a whole body massage with their baby. The approach is designed with respect for the baby's needs and learning pace.

### Respectful, Gradual Learning

It can take time for babies to learn to accept new touch and massage techniques. Out of respect for the baby, the massage is introduced in bite-sized chunks that the baby can tolerate and the parent can remember. By the end of 5 weeks, parents feel well supported to massage their baby at home.

### Doll-Based Teaching

Instructors never massage someone else's baby. Instead, they use a doll to teach parents how to use nurturing touch and massage with their baby. This approach empowers parents to apply the techniques themselves while maintaining appropriate boundaries and respecting the parent-baby relationship.

## Ongoing Support for Practitioners

IAIM NZ provides robust, ongoing support for certified instructors (CIMIs) to maintain skills, foster community, and ensure professional growth. Support is integrated into membership benefits, emphasizing peer networking and accessible professional development.

### Annual Membership

Membership grants access to all resources, including free quarterly events. Lapsed members can renew at any time to reconnect with the community and access ongoing support.

### Stroke Review Sessions

Four free online livestream sessions are offered per year, covering topics such as Special & Additional Needs, Cues, Crying, and Relaxation. These sessions are facilitated by experienced trainers and allow for practice, knowledge refresh, experience-sharing, and networking. They are ideal for instructors needing reminders or guidance, and provide opportunities to reconnect with the community.

### Refresher Days

Comprehensive online refresher events are held annually, covering detailed reviews of strokes, cues, behavioral states, and parent connections, plus peer discussions. These events are included in membership benefits through the integrated Stroke Review program. Additional resources such as updated manuals and reference books are available to support ongoing professional development.

### Mentoring and Direct Support

Direct trainer support is available via email for inquiries before or after training. Follow-up feedback on assignments and coursework is provided, along with access to pre-reading materials and training notes. Community-building through regular sessions encourages reconnection and skill updates.

### Professional Development

Regular events prepare instructors for advanced teaching and workplace contributions, supporting long-term engagement with IAIM's global network. These mechanisms ensure instructors remain confident, connected, and current in their practice.

## Code of Conduct and Ethical Guidelines

IAIM NZ embeds ethics within its philosophy and training, prioritizing safety, respect, and non-intrusive practices. Core ethical standards are outlined in training materials and operational principles:

### Key Ethical Principles

**Non-Contact with Others' Babies**: Instructors must never massage another person's baby. Demonstrations use dolls only to model strokes, empowering parents to apply techniques themselves. This upholds respectful, nurturing communication and avoids boundary violations.

**Respect and Inclusivity**: Training emphasizes valuing all children—typically developing, premature, and special needs—and families, promoting loving touch without judgment. Objectives include understanding diverse contributions in workplaces and maintaining high professional standards.

**Professional Standards**: Certification requires ethical practice alignment with IAIM's global curriculum, including behavioral cues awareness and relaxation promotion. Privacy policies reinforce data ethics through minimal collection and access rights.

**Ongoing Accountability**: Membership and refresher participation support standards through community accountability and skill maintenance, ensuring practitioners remain current and ethical in their approach.

These guidelines foster a safe, empowering environment, with trainer oversight ensuring compliance. For deeper understanding of ethical principles, practitioners reference IAIM's foundational texts like McClure's handbook.
