
1. Overall look and feel (typical web styling)

Visual style
- Calm, “wellness” aesthetic
- Light backgrounds (mostly white or very pale neutrals) with soft accent colours.
- A “perinatal / yoga / holistic health” vibe is common, especially where massage is one of several maternity services.  ￼
- Photography and imagery
- Hero images of relaxed babies, skin-to-skin contact, or parent-baby interaction.
- Small, intimate class photos or “floor mat” setups, especially on course pages.  ￼
- Logos of IAIM / IMIS or similar bodies where relevant, often on About/IAIM pages or footer.  ￼
- Layout patterns
- Standard top navigation bar with 5–8 menu items; responsive single-column layout on mobile.
- Clear “Book now” / “Pricing & booking” buttons near the top of key pages.  ￼
- Content organised into short sections with H2/H3 headings and bullet lists (especially for benefits and practical info).  ￼
- Tone of language
- Warm, reassuring and low-jargon: “calm, confidence, connection”, “nurturing touch”, “beautiful way to bond with your baby”.  ￼
- Emphasis on empowerment (“learn the skills”, “feel more confident”) and community (“meet other parents”, “small groups”).  ￼

2. Typical information architecture (site structure)

From sites such as Serene Pregnancy (NZ), Blissful Bubs (NZ), Gentle Hands Baby Massage (AU), and Centre for Perinatal Health & Parenting (AU), a fairly standard pattern shows up.  ￼

2.1 Main navigation

Common top-level menu items:
- Home
- Classes / Baby Massage / Infant Massage
- About / Your Instructor
- Benefits or a benefits section on the main baby-massage page
- Pricing & Booking / Book Now
- FAQs
- Testimonials
- Contact
- Sometimes Shop (for gift vouchers, online courses) and Blog/Articles.  ￼

Many practitioners are part of broader perinatal or massage businesses, so infant massage often sits under a Services menu alongside yoga, birth preparation, breastfeeding support, etc.  ￼

⸻

3. Key content sections (by page)

3.1 Home page

Typical elements:
- Hero block
- Short headline: e.g. “Baby Massage Classes” or “Nurture Your Baby Through Touch”.
- One-sentence value proposition: bond, soothe, support development.  ￼
- Primary CTA: “Book a class”, “View upcoming courses”.
- Snapshot of benefits
- Bullet list summarising benefits for baby (sleep, digestion, relaxation, body awareness) and parent (confidence, bonding, reduced stress).  ￼
- Trust elements
- Brief note about credentials: IAIM-certified, IMIS-trained, health-professional background.  ￼
- Quick links
- Next course dates / timetable.
- Gift vouchers (where offered).  ￼

⸻

3.2 Baby / Infant massage page (core service page)

Across NZ and AU sites this page usually carries most of the substance.  ￼

Content sections typically include:
	1.	What is baby/infant massage?
- Short description focused on nurturing touch, communication, and bonding.
- Often framed as a parent-education class, not treatment done to the baby.
	2.	Benefits
- Commonly organised into categories (e.g. Interaction, Stimulation, Relief, Relaxation), mirroring IAIM material.  ￼
- Separate bullet lists for:
- Benefits for baby (sleep, colic/gas relief, relaxation, body awareness, nervous system development).  ￼
- Benefits for parents/caregivers (confidence, understanding cues, reduced anxiety, enjoyable one-to-one time).  ￼
	3.	How classes are run
- Duration and structure: typically 4–5 week programmes, 60–90 minutes per week.  ￼
- Progressive learning: new strokes each week, recap of previous strokes to build a full-body routine.  ￼
- Format options:
- Small group classes at a clinic / parents’ centre.  ￼
- Private in-home sessions or 1:1/1:2 parent support.  ￼
- Clear statement that only parents massage their baby; the instructor demonstrates on a doll.  ￼
	4.	Who it’s for
- Age range: often “from newborn to around 6–12 months”, sometimes broader for special needs/older babies.  ￼
- Who can attend: mothers, fathers, partners, occasionally grandparents or other caregivers.  ￼
	5.	Course logistics
- Venue/location and environment description (warm, quiet space; mats provided; sometimes outdoor lawn in good weather).  ￼
- What’s included: oil, handbook or notes, refreshments, small group discussion.  ￼
- Pricing and what the fee covers (e.g. 5-week course fee, 4-week clinic‐based series, options for individual sessions).  ￼
	6.	Safety and consent messaging
- Emphasis on reading baby’s cues and respecting “no” signals.  ￼
- Clarification that massage is supportive, not a medical treatment; suggestion to consult a health professional where needed.
- “Ask baby’s permission” narrative appears or is implied on IAIM-aligned sites.  ￼
	7.	Calls to action
- “Book now”, “View upcoming dates”, “Email/phone to reserve a place”.  ￼

⸻

3.3 About / Your Instructor

Typical components:
- Instructor bio
- Short personal story, often from a doula, midwife, yoga teacher, counsellor, or massage therapist background.  ￼
- Emphasis on passion for supporting parents, own experience as a parent.
- Training & credentials
- Named training provider: IAIM, Infant Massage Information Service (IMIS), Bump Yoga, or similar.  ￼
- Any additional professional memberships (e.g. massage associations, perinatal associations, first aid/CPR).  ￼
- Philosophy statement
- Respectful, baby-led, consent-based, attachment-oriented language.  ￼

⸻

3.4 Dedicated “Benefits” / “Why massage your baby?” pages

On some sites benefits get their own page or a long section:
- Detailed lists of physical, emotional, and relational benefits, often 8–12 items long for both baby and caregiver.  ￼
- Short research references or nods to “evidence-based” / “supported by research”, sometimes linking to IAIM or broader parenting resources.  ￼

⸻

3.5 FAQs and practical info

Typical FAQ topics:
- When can we start?
- Can both parents attend?
- What if my baby cries or sleeps during class?
- What oil do you use? Can I bring my own?  ￼
- What if my baby has reflux / colic / medical conditions?
- What do we need to bring (nappies, blanket, change of clothes, etc.)?

FAQs usually sit either as a separate page or as an accordion section below the main class description.  ￼

⸻

3.6 Pricing, vouchers, and booking
- Pricing tables or short sections on the class page or linked via “Pricing & Booking”.  ￼
- Gift vouchers sold through a Shop page or product page (“Baby Massage voucher”, with course description and price).  ￼
- Booking methods:
- Embedded booking forms or schedulers.
- Or simple “call / email reception” instructions, especially for clinics and centres.  ￼

⸻

3.7 Testimonials and social proof
- Short quotes from parents about:
- Feeling more confident
- Baby sleeping better or being more settled
- Enjoying the group environment
- Sometimes a separate Testimonials page, sometimes just a section on the home or baby-massage page.  ￼
- Links to Facebook / Instagram pages with photos and occasional course updates.  ￼

⸻

4. Patterns you can safely generalise

From this cross-section of NZ and Australian sites, the “typical” infant-massage practitioner presence:
- Lives inside a gentle, wellness-oriented brand (often perinatal or holistic-care focused).
- Uses soft, calm visual styling with lots of whitespace and baby–parent imagery.
- Centres content on:
- Benefits (often IAIM-style categories)
- Course structure (multi-week, small group, 60–90 min)
- Parental empowerment (you learn, you massage, we use dolls)
- Safety, consent, and baby-led pace

If you’re designing or auditing a site, that outline above is a decent “minimal viable structure” for looking convincingly aligned with what NZ/AU parents encounter elsewhere, while leaving plenty of room for your own brand voice and design choices.
